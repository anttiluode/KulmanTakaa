Your first corner-camera test

Hide the phone.
Watch the floor.

Put an opaque edge between the camera and a bright phone. Keep the floor beside that edge visible: it is where light from the hidden phone can reach the camera.

A camera deviceA second phoneTall cardboard or a boxA matte floor or sheet of paper
01 / ARRANGE THE BENCH

The wall must block the direct view.

Seen from above. The edge should be taller than the camera lens. Start in a dim room with the phone close to the corner.

Swipe the diagram sideways to see the whole arrangement.

The orange path is light: phone → floor → camera. The dashed line checks whether the phone is physically hidden. Drawing is schematic, not to scale.

Give the edge a real job.

From the lens position, the wall must interrupt the straight line to the phone. Also check for visible reflections of its screen.

Leave the floor exposed.

A light, matte patch beside the corner should stay in view. Keep your hands and the cardboard out of this patch.

Use a bright actor first.

The phone’s emitted light is an easier starting test than light reflected from a person in another room.

✓Correct arrangement: the camera cannot see the phone directly, but can see the floor receiving its light.

02 / SELECT THE FLOOR GEOMETRY

Three clicks, in this order.

In KulmanTakaa, choose Camera, then Set corner. Practise here by selecting the numbered points.

Swipe the camera illustration sideways if a point is out of view.

Camera-view illustration. In your actual image, the locations differ. Follow the physical edge and floor, not these example pixel positions.

1

Foot of the corner

Where the vertical edge touches the floor. This is the centre of the angular wedge.

2

Along the wall’s base

Follow the line where the wall meets the floor, toward the camera’s side. Click on that line.

3

Far end of the floor patch

Click out across the visible floor beside the edge. This sets the other direction and the patch’s reach.

Start with point 1: the corner’s foot.

03 / TRY THE MEASUREMENT

Learn the room, then move the light.

  1. On the hidden phone, open actor.html, use landscape orientation and high screen brightness. Choose Off.
  2. Keep the camera still. In the instrument, try Lock exposure, then Learn empty room.
  3. Switch the phone to Slots. Record about a minute and inspect the recovered waterfall and score.
  4. Switch the phone to Still or Off, restart the score and record another minute. A fresh score should not claim tracking of the moving schedule.

WHAT WOULD COUNT?

Recovery must follow the optical signal.

The “What changed” panel is amplified background subtraction. The narrow “actor says” stripe is a clock-generated reference. Neither panel alone proves that the camera recovered hidden motion.

For stronger evidence, recover independently chosen hidden movements from a recording of the floor, then compare with a separate record of the actor’s movements.

Block the light path to the floor and repeat. Also cover the entire lens: fresh tracking should stop after the transient. Old waterfall rows and the clock reference can remain visible.

This guide explains the arrangement. A successful real-camera reconstruction is still an experimental result to establish.
